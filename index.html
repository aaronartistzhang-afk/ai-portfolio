Ask me anything…

A creator doesn't want a dashboard. They ask "am I still in it?" — and the answer has to come back as a decision, not a number.

1Meet the intent, not the words. "Can I still make it?" is a worry before it is a query. Answer the worry first, or nothing after it lands.
2Diagnose against something checkable. Rank, the exact gap, and where the points came from — so the advice can be argued with instead of believed.
3End on one doable move. Not "engage more" — a specific action, sized to the time actually left.

L1 · ASK · own the meaning

Same data. Same weeks. Opposite answer.

AI found the culprit in seconds — clean, confident, and wrong. It had counted the wrong denominator. Count only the eligible base, and the same weeks read a steady 91–96%. The −19.6% was a definition pretending to be reality.

Fast confidence is not correctness.
one metric · two denominators — which one is real?

L3 · EMBED · own the workflow

The loop runs — and closes — without me starting it.

Something happens→ AI drafts→ I approve→ Task opened→ Tracked to closure
weekly reviewillustrative mockup
🧭 Weekly on-call digest
grouped by severity · drafted for approval · tracked to closure
Open threads grouped by severity→ the highest-risk items surface first
One escalation drafted for approval→ nothing escalates without my sign-off
💡 every item is tracked until it closes

I don't kick this off. The workflow watches, drafts, and — once I approve — opens the task and follows it to closure. This is a process I already knew how to run: I wrote the steps down, and now it runs them. I hold the gate; the loop does the legwork.

gates · escalation · closure
L4 · GOVERN · own the system

I stopped telling it what to look at — I told it what I care about.

L3 · embed
I designed the steps. It runs a process I already knew how to run, and stops at my gate.
vs
L4 · govern
I never wrote the steps. It decides what deserves attention out of an open world — in a field I don't know. I set only the goal and the guardrails.

L5 · TEACH · own the method

The method that caught my −19.6% — now anyone can install it.

I froze the whole "who counts?" checking method into a Skill — a reusable instruction pack, an SOP for AI. A teammate who never saw the trap now gets the corrected answer. Ownership stopped living only in my head.

what it took to ship one safely

A second AI reviewed it and found 6 things I'd gotten wrong. It was tested again and again. A human still had to approve it before it could go live.

the trap at the top

Package your first Skill and you feel unstoppable. That's the most dangerous moment — mastery is a moving target.

Demo → production is still a very long road.
